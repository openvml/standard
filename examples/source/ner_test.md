### Character Alias Reference

* **Mira** — the little singer, college student, blonde girl, fair-haired girl, the young performer.
* **Clara** — the girl with glasses, top student, college scholar, the nerd, the bespectacled student.
* **Bran** — the old boatswain, sea dog, veteran sailor, old deckhand, tattooed bruiser.
* **Gareth** — the mustached cook, chef, kitchen master, the cook, owner of the galley.
* **Victor** — the grim bouncer, big guy, heavyset guard, enforcer, the man keeping order.
* **Selene** — the demanding diva, model, brunette, tall fashionista, glamorous woman.

---

### Opening Scene

In the dim back room of a harbor tavern called **The Rusty Anchor**, an unlikely group had gathered around a corner table.

**Mira** nervously plucked at the strings of her silent guitar, feeling the jealous stare of **Clara** behind her. The bespectacled student methodically cleaned her glasses while checking something in a battered notebook.

A little farther away, **Bran** sat lazily sipping cheap rum. His tattooed fingers tapped out an irregular rhythm against the wooden table.

From the kitchen came the clatter of pots and pans. **Gareth** was arguing with a supplier again, his booming voice carrying through the entire tavern.

At the bar, **Victor** looked bored, tossing a shiny coin into the air and catching it without even looking.

A few minutes later, **Selene** swept into the room with the confidence of someone who expected everyone to stop and watch her. Her bright makeup contrasted sharply with her strict business suit.

### Dialogue

1. — If we don't start the show in five minutes, the audience is going to tear this place apart, — squeaked **the little singer**, pushing a loose strand of blonde hair back into her ponytail.

2. — Calm down, — replied **the girl with glasses** dryly without looking up from her notes. — According to my calculations, the crowd won't reach its peak for another thirty minutes.

3. **The old boatswain** gave a low chuckle through his mustache and finished his rum. — Your calculations forgot that three cargo ships were unloaded at the harbor today. There's nowhere left to stand.

4. — And who's going to pay for all the broken dishes? — shouted **the mustached cook** from behind the kitchen curtain, waving a wooden ladle threateningly.

5. A lazy voice came from the corner of the bar. — Relax, chef. My people are controlling the entrance. We won't let anyone extra inside, — said **the grim bouncer**, catching the falling coin in his palm.

6. At that moment the doors swung open, and **the demanding diva** entered the room with a theatrical sigh. — What a dump. You can practically smell the cheap beer and stale ale.

7. — Oh, look who's finally here, — sneered **the singer**, deliberately turning her back toward the wall. — We've only been waiting for you for an hour, Your Highness.

8. — Traffic, darling, — said **the model**, waving dismissively while inspecting her flawless manicure. — Besides, my time is worth more than this little concert of yours.

9. **The top student** finally closed her notebook and gave the newcomer a stern look. — We're supposed to work as a team. Your ego is destroying the entire schedule.

10. — We've heard your lectures before, — snorted **the brunette**, walking toward the mirror beside the stage. — Where's our sponsor?

11. — Our distinguished investor, — answered **the sea dog**, — is currently calculating the losses from the last storm. So we'll have to manage on our own.

12. — Wonderful, — muttered **the chef**, returning to the stove. — That means I'll probably miss another bonus for the banquet.

13. — Stop whining, — growled **the tattooed bruiser**, getting up from his chair. — Hey, bartender, pour me another one before these strings snap.

14. **The big guy** behind the counter silently filled a glass and pushed it toward the musician. — That's the last one on the house. After that, you pay full price.

15. — Hey, **the college student**, — called **the tall fashionista** from the stage, — hand me that microphone. And be careful. Don't dirty the cable.

16. **The fair-haired girl** flushed with indignation. — What, did your hands fall off? Get it yourself. I'm not your servant!

17. — Ladies, break it up, — **the man keeping order** said lazily. — If you start a fight, I'll throw both of you out without severance pay.

18. — Fine, — sighed **the bespectacled student**. — Guitarist ready. Vocals ready. We start on the first signal.

19. From the kitchen came the final chord of **the kitchen master's** culinary symphony. — Good riddance! Just stay away from my serving counter!

20. — Well then, here we go, — grinned **the old sailor**, taking one final sip and winking at **the fair-haired girl**.
